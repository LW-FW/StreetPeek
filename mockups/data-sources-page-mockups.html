Data & methodology

Where we get our data, and how we turn it into your report

Every figure on a StreetPeek report traces back to an official, public source. Here's exactly which ones, how fresh they are, and where they fall short — so you can check our workings, not just trust them.

StreetPeek is a research tool, not professional advice. It's not a substitute for a solicitor's local searches, a RICS survey, or independent legal advice before you buy. Always get the proper professional checks done — use us to know what to ask them.

We built StreetPeek because pulling this picture together yourself means separate trips to a police API, a planning portal, a schools register and an OS map — and most people don't have time to do that for every house they view.

So we do the legwork and hand you the summary. But a summary is only useful if you can see underneath it, so this page exists to show our workings: what each part of your report is built from, how current it is, and where the gaps are. Dig into the primary source yourself, and use it to learn what to check on the next home even if you never open StreetPeek again. We hope you'll come back because it's convenient — not because it's the only way to find this out.

The sources, one by one

Everything below is public-sector or open-community data. Nothing is scraped from forums, estate agent copy, or guesswork.

Postcode & boundariesLive lookup

Turns the postcode you search into a map point, plus its ward, district and constituency.

Source: postcodes.io, built on ONS (Office for National Statistics) postcode data · queried fresh on every search

CrimeLive lookup

Street-level crimes reported near the address, grouped by category.

Source: data.police.uk, the Home Office's official crime API · figures are for the latest month the police have published, which typically lags today's date by 4–8 weeks, and locations are anonymised to a nearby point rather than an exact address

Planning applications & brownfield landCoverage varies

Nearby planning applications, brownfield housing sites, and infrastructure projects.

Source: planning.data.gov.uk, the government's national planning data platform, which aggregates what individual councils choose to publish in a standard format

  • Some councils publish rich, current data; others publish very little — a "no results" here can mean "clear" or "this council hasn't published," and today we can't always tell you which
  • We're separately building direct connections into individual council planning portals to close these gaps — not all of the 300+ we're mapping are wired in yet, so treat today's coverage as improving rather than complete
Environment & heritage constraintsCoverage varies

Flood risk zones, conservation areas, green belt, listed buildings, tree preservation orders, ancient woodland and similar designations.

Source: planning.data.gov.uk, same platform and same council-by-council coverage caveat as above

Local amenitiesCommunity-mapped

Nearby GPs, dentists, pharmacies, supermarkets and gyms.

Source: OpenStreetMap, via the Overpass API · maintained by volunteer mappers — coverage is generally strong in the UK but can be patchy or out of date in a specific street

Schools & nurseriesRefreshed monthly

Nearby schools and nurseries, their type, and their latest Ofsted rating.

Sources: GIAS (the DfE's official schools register), Ofsted's published inspection outcomes for schools and childcare providers, and ONS postcode data for locations

  • Ofsted's published figures can lag the actual inspection by a few weeks
  • Schools are mid-transition to Ofsted's new rating system, so most schools still carry a rating under the old one — we show whichever is current and label which system it's from
  • Independent schools appear in the register but aren't Ofsted-rated (they're inspected by the ISI instead)

How the area score is calculated

Every area starts at a baseline of 7.0, then is adjusted:

  • Crime: −2 if over 200 crimes/month, −1 if over 100, +1 if under 30
  • Flood risk: −1 if a flood risk zone is nearby
  • Conservation area: +0.5 if nearby
  • Brownfield housing: +0.5 if a site is nearby

The score is capped between 1 and 10. It's a rough compass, not a verdict — it only reflects the handful of signals above, not everything that makes a place a good fit for you.

Known limitations

  • Council-published planning & environment data is uneven — silence isn't proof of "clear"
  • Crime figures are roughly a month or more behind today, and locations are approximate by design
  • Amenity data quality depends on volunteer mapping in that specific area
  • The area score reflects only crime, flood, conservation and brownfield signals — not schools, transport, or amenities yet
